Keyboard lab

Identifies your gaming keyboard (for example a Xinmeng "Crazy Snake") and shows how it talks to the computer, as a first step towards controlling its lights.

Read-only. This page can't send anything to the keyboard: it only reads and listens. It can't change, break or reset your keyboard. Gaming keyboards save settings in their own memory, so sending untested commands is a risk we won't take until we know more.

1Connect

Click Connect. Chrome shows a list of every USB device it can see. Pick your keyboard (its name may be something like "Gaming Keyboard", "USB Keyboard" or a code). If there are several entries with the same name, connect them all: click Connect again for each one.

Not sure which is the keyboard? Unplug it, click Connect and look at the list, then close it, plug the keyboard back in and look again. The new entries are your keyboard.

2Read stored values

Reads every "feature report" the keyboard offers. These are often where settings live. This only reads.

3Change the lights with the keyboard and watch

Most of these keyboards change lighting with key combinations. Try these one at a time and watch both the keyboard and the log below:

  • A key with a light-bulb or sun symbol (often Scroll Lock or Pause) on its own
  • Fn + Ins, Home, PgUp, Del, End, PgDn (lighting modes on many boards)
  • Fn + ↑ / ↓ (brightness) and Fn + ← / → (speed)
  • Fn + 1 to 9, and Fn + \ (colour on some boards)

These combinations are common on budget boards, but every model differs. They're safe to try: at worst the lights change or a key like Windows lock toggles (press the same combination again to undo). If the keyboard sends a message to the PC when the lights change, it shows up in the log as IN. That would be a big clue.
